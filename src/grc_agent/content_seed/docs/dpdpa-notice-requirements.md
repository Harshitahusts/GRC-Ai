A DPDPA notice must **accompany or precede** every request for consent (Section 5(1)). The notice is how people become "informed", which consent requires under [Section 6](/docs/dpdpa-consent-and-legitimate-uses).

## What the notice must tell people (Section 5(1))

- the **personal data** you'll process and the **purpose** of processing
- **how to exercise their rights** to withdraw consent (Section 6(4)) and to have grievances redressed (Section 13)
- **how to complain to the Data Protection Board**

## Format under the DPDP Rules

The Rules add that the notice should:

- be understandable **on its own**, not buried in other information
- be in **clear and plain language**
- give an **itemised description** of the personal data
- state the **specific purpose** and the specific goods, services or uses it enables
- include a **link or method** to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board

## Common mistakes

- A single privacy policy page linked in the footer, but nothing shown at the point of collection.
- Purposes described so broadly ("to improve our services") that consent isn't specific.
- No working way to withdraw consent, or a withdrawal process harder than signing up.
- Notices that mention rights but not how to complain to the Board.

## Consent given before the Act (Section 5(2))

If someone gave consent before the Act commenced, you must give them a notice **as soon as reasonably practicable**. You can keep processing until they withdraw consent.

## Language (Section 5(3))

People must be able to read the notice in **English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution**. Offer the languages your customers actually use.

## Notice checklist

- [ ] Shown at every point where personal data is collected: website forms, apps, onboarding, offline forms that get digitised
- [ ] Lists each category of personal data
- [ ] States each purpose separately
- [ ] Explains how to withdraw consent, with a working link
- [ ] Explains how to raise a grievance, and your response time
- [ ] Explains how to complain to the Data Protection Board
- [ ] Available in the languages your users need
- [ ] Sent to existing customers whose consent predates the Act
