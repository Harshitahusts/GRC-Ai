Under **Section 8(6)**, when a personal data breach happens, a Data Fiduciary must inform the **Data Protection Board** and **each affected Data Principal**. Failing to do so can cost up to **₹200 crore**.

A **personal data breach** is any unauthorised processing, or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to personal data, that compromises its confidentiality, integrity or availability. There's **no minimum size**: one affected person is enough.

## Telling affected people

Under the Rules, inform each affected Data Principal **without delay**, in a concise, clear and plain way, through their user account or the contact details you have. Include:

- what happened: the nature, extent, timing and location of the breach
- the likely consequences for them
- what you've done and are doing to reduce the risk
- what they can do to protect themselves
- a contact person who can answer their questions

## Telling the Data Protection Board

Under the Rules, there are two steps:

1. **Without delay**: a description of the breach, including its nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact.
2. **Within 72 hours** of becoming aware of it (or longer if the Board allows): a detailed report with the facts, the circumstances and cause, the measures taken, findings about the person who caused it if known, remedial steps, and the notices sent to affected people.

## Build a breach playbook before you need it

- Name an **incident owner** and a deputy, with phone numbers.
- Define how staff report a suspected breach, and to whom.
- Write the containment steps for your main systems.
- Prepare **notification templates** for the Board and for affected people.
- Keep a **breach log** with times, decisions and notices, for every incident, even ones you decide aren't breaches.
- Require your **Data Processors** to tell you about breaches promptly, in their contracts.
- Rehearse it once a year.

**CERT-In**: separate rules require many organisations to report cyber incidents to CERT-In within **6 hours**. A single incident can trigger both regimes, so plan for both.
