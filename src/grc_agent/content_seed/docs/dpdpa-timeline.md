The DPDPA timeline runs in phases rather than switching everything on at once. The Act was passed in 2023, the Rules followed in 2025, and the main obligations bite in 2027. These are the key dates.

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 11 August 2023 | The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 receives Presidential assent. |
| November 2025 | MeitY notifies the **DPDP Rules, 2025**. Provisions setting up the **Data Protection Board** take effect immediately. |
| November 2026 (about 12 months later) | Rules on the **registration and obligations of Consent Managers** take effect. |
| **13 May 2027** (about 18 months later) | The **main obligations** take effect: notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, erasure, children's data, SDF duties and Data Principal rights. |

## What this means

- The deadline is fixed. Compliance programmes typically take 6 to 12 months, so the time to start is now.
- Contracts signed today with vendors will still be running in May 2027. Add data processing terms when you sign or renew.
- Consent collected before May 2027 doesn't have to be collected again, but you must send a [notice](/docs/dpdpa-notice-requirements) to those people as soon as reasonably practicable.

## What happens if you miss it

From May 2027 the Data Protection Board can act on complaints and breach reports, and [penalties](/docs/dpdpa-penalties) reach ₹250 crore. The Board weighs how seriously an organisation tried to comply, so documented progress matters even if not everything is finished.

## A realistic plan

1. **Now**: map your personal data, systems and vendors. Name an owner.
2. **Next 3 months**: gap assessment against the Act and Rules. See the [checklist](/docs/dpdpa-compliance-checklist).
3. **Next 6 months**: fix notices and consent, sign processing agreements, write a [breach playbook](/docs/dpdpa-personal-data-breach), set retention periods.
4. **Before May 2027**: test rights requests and breach response, train staff, and keep records of it all.

> Check the exact commencement dates in the gazette notifications before quoting them to a client.
