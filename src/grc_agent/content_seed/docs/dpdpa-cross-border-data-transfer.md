**Section 16** of the DPDPA governs cross-border data transfer. It lets personal data be transferred outside India **unless the government restricts** the destination. That makes India's approach more permissive than regimes that require an adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses for every transfer, but the restrictions can change by notification at any time, so the position has to be re-checked rather than settled once.

## The negative-list approach (Section 16(1))

The government may, by notification, **restrict transfers** of personal data to any country or territory outside India. Transfers anywhere not restricted are allowed under the DPDPA, subject to the rest of the Act. This is sometimes called a "blacklist" or negative-list approach, the opposite of an approved-countries list.

## Stricter sector rules still apply (Section 16(2))

If another Indian law gives a **higher degree of protection** or restricts transfers further, that law prevails. For example, **RBI** requires payment system data to be stored only in India, and sector regulators in insurance and telecom have their own rules.

## Under the DPDP Rules

The Rules add that transfers are subject to any **requirements the government specifies** about making personal data available to a foreign State or its agencies. [Significant Data Fiduciaries](/docs/dpdpa-significant-data-fiduciary) may also be required to keep certain specified personal data **within India**.

## What to check

Most businesses transfer data abroad without thinking of it as a "transfer": Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, AWS, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Zoom and similar tools often store data outside India.

- [ ] List every tool and vendor that stores or accesses personal data.
- [ ] Record where each one stores data (country or region).
- [ ] Check each destination against any current government restriction.
- [ ] Check sector rules (RBI, IRDAI, SEBI and others) for stricter requirements.
- [ ] Make sure each vendor has a [valid processing contract](/docs/dpdpa-data-fiduciary-obligations).
- [ ] Re-check whenever the government issues a new notification.

Ask clients **which tools they use**, not whether they transfer data abroad. Most will say no, and be wrong.
