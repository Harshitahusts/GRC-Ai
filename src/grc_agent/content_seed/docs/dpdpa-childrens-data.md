Under the DPDPA a **child** is anyone who hasn't completed **18 years** of age. **Section 9** adds duties for processing children's data, and breaking them can cost up to **₹200 crore**.

## Verifiable parental consent (Section 9(1))

Before processing a child's personal data, a Data Fiduciary must obtain the **verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian**. The same applies to a person with a disability who has a lawful guardian.

The Rules describe how to verify it: take reasonable steps to check that the person consenting is an identifiable adult, using reliable identity and age details already available to you, or details or a virtual token issued by an authorised entity, such as through DigiLocker.

## No harm to well-being (Section 9(2))

Don't process personal data in a way that's likely to have a **detrimental effect on a child's well-being**.

## No tracking, monitoring or targeted ads (Section 9(3))

Don't undertake **tracking or behavioural monitoring** of children, or **targeted advertising** directed at them.

## Exemptions

The government can exempt certain classes of Data Fiduciary or purposes from Section 9(1) and 9(3). The Rules list some, for example certain processing by healthcare providers, educational institutions and childcare providers, limited to what's needed for their purpose. Check the exact conditions before relying on one.

## What to do

- Find out whether **any** users could be under 18. Don't assume they aren't.
- Add an age check at sign-up, and a parental consent step for under-18s.
- Keep a record of how each parent's identity and consent were verified.
- Switch off behavioural tracking and ad targeting for child accounts.
- Review features such as leaderboards, public profiles and messaging for harm to well-being.

EdTech, gaming and social platforms are the most exposed. See the [compliance checklist](/docs/dpdpa-compliance-checklist) and [penalties](/docs/dpdpa-penalties).
