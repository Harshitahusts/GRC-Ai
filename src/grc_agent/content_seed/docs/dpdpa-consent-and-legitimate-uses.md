DPDPA consent rules start from **Section 4**: personal data may be processed only for a **lawful purpose**, and only on one of two grounds:

1. the Data Principal's **consent** (Section 6), or
2. a **legitimate use** listed in Section 7.

## What valid consent looks like (Section 6)

Consent must be **free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous**, given by a **clear affirmative action**. It must be limited to the personal data needed for the stated purpose (Section 6(1)).

In practice:

- use an **unticked** checkbox or an explicit "I agree" for each purpose; pre-ticked boxes and silence don't count
- don't bundle consent for marketing into your terms of service
- ask only for data you need for the purpose
- give a [notice](/docs/dpdpa-notice-requirements) with, or before, the request for consent

Any part of a consent that breaks the Act is invalid to that extent (Section 6(2)).

## Withdrawing consent (Section 6(4) to 6(6))

People can withdraw consent at any time, and withdrawing must be **as easy as giving it**. After withdrawal, the Data Fiduciary must stop processing within a reasonable time, and make its Data Processors stop too, unless the law requires or allows the processing to continue. Processing done before the withdrawal stays lawful.

## Proving consent (Section 6(10))

If there's a dispute, the **Data Fiduciary must prove** that notice was given and consent was obtained. Keep consent records: what was shown, what was agreed, and when.

## Consent Managers (Section 6(7) to 6(9))

People can give, manage and withdraw consent through a **Consent Manager** registered with the Board. The Rules set conditions for registration, including being incorporated in India.

## Legitimate uses: processing without consent (Section 7)

You don't need consent when processing is for one of these uses (summarised):

- a purpose for which the person **voluntarily provided** the data, where they haven't said they don't consent
- the State providing a subsidy, benefit, service, licence or permit
- the State performing a function under law, or in the interest of sovereignty, integrity or security of India
- complying with a **legal obligation** to disclose information to the State
- complying with a **court judgment or order**
- responding to a **medical emergency** involving a threat to life or health
- providing medical treatment or health services during an **epidemic** or threat to public health
- ensuring safety or providing services during a **disaster** or breakdown of public order
- **employment purposes**, or safeguarding the employer from loss or liability, such as preventing corporate espionage

Legitimate uses are narrow. If you're relying on one, write down which one and why.
