The government can notify any Data Fiduciary, or class of them, as a **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)** under **Section 10**. SDFs carry extra duties, and breaking them can cost up to **₹150 crore**.

## How SDFs are chosen (Section 10(1))

The government weighs factors including:

- the **volume and sensitivity** of personal data processed
- the **risk to the rights** of Data Principals
- the potential impact on the **sovereignty and integrity of India**
- the risk to **electoral democracy**
- **security of the State** and **public order**

There's no automatic threshold. An organisation becomes an SDF when it is notified.

## Extra duties (Section 10(2))

An SDF must:

1. appoint a **Data Protection Officer** who is **based in India**, represents the SDF under the Act, reports to its board of directors or similar governing body, and is the point of contact for grievances
2. appoint an **independent data auditor** to evaluate its compliance
3. carry out a periodic **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)**
4. carry out a periodic **audit**
5. take other measures the government prescribes

## Under the DPDP Rules

The Rules add that an SDF must:

- complete a DPIA and an audit **once every 12 months**, and give the Board a report of the significant observations
- check that any **algorithmic software** it uses isn't likely to pose a risk to Data Principals' rights
- make sure certain personal data specified by the government, on a committee's recommendation, **isn't transferred outside India**

## What a DPIA covers

A Data Protection Impact Assessment describes the rights of the people whose data is processed, the purpose of processing, an assessment of the risks to their rights, and the measures to manage those risks. It should be updated whenever processing changes significantly.

## Should you prepare?

Large platforms with sensitive or high-volume data, in health, finance, social media or e-commerce, should assume they could be notified and start with a DPO and a DPIA.
