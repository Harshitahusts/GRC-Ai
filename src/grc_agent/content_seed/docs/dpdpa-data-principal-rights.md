**Chapter III** of the DPDPA sets out Data Principal rights: four rights for individuals, and **Section 15** gives them duties.

## Right to access information (Section 11)

On request, a Data Principal can get from the Data Fiduciary:

- a **summary** of their personal data being processed and the processing activities
- the **identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors** the data has been shared with, and a description of the data shared
- any other information the Rules prescribe

This right applies where the person gave consent or voluntarily provided the data (the legitimate use in Section 7(a)).

## Right to correction and erasure (Section 12)

A Data Principal can ask you to **correct, complete or update** inaccurate or incomplete data, and to **erase** data, unless you must keep it to serve the specified purpose or to comply with a law.

## Right of grievance redressal (Section 13)

People have the right to **readily available means of grievance redressal** from the Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager. They must **use this route first** before complaining to the Data Protection Board. The Rules require Data Fiduciaries to publish how to raise a grievance and to respond within a set period, capped at **90 days**.

## Right to nominate (Section 14)

A Data Principal can **nominate** someone to exercise their rights if they die or become incapable.

## How to handle requests

- Publish how to make a request: an email address, web form or in-app option.
- Verify the requester's identity before acting.
- Log each request, the response and the dates.
- Set an internal deadline well inside the maximum response period.

## Duties of Data Principals (Section 15)

People must not impersonate others, must not suppress material information when giving data for official documents, must not register **false or frivolous grievances**, and must give only verifiably authentic information when exercising the right to correction or erasure. Breaching these duties can lead to a penalty of up to **₹10,000**.
