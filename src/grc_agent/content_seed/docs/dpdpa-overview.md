The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA)** is India's law on how organisations collect and use personal data in digital form. It was passed by Parliament in August 2023. The **DPDP Rules, 2025**, notified by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) in November 2025, fill in the operational detail, and most obligations take full effect in **May 2027**. See the [implementation timeline](/docs/dpdpa-timeline).

## Who the DPDPA applies to

The Act applies to the processing of **digital personal data** within India, whether it was collected digitally or collected on paper and later digitised (Section 3). It also applies to processing outside India if it's connected to offering goods or services to people in India.

It does **not** apply to:

- personal data an individual uses for personal or domestic purposes
- personal data that the person themselves, or someone legally obliged to, has made publicly available

## The key roles

| Role | Who it is |
|---|---|
| Data Principal | The individual the data is about. For a child, this includes the parent or lawful guardian. |
| Data Fiduciary | The organisation that decides why and how personal data is processed. Most obligations fall here. |
| Data Processor | An organisation that processes data on a Data Fiduciary's behalf, such as a cloud or payroll provider. |
| Significant Data Fiduciary | A Data Fiduciary the government notifies as higher risk. It has extra duties. |
| Consent Manager | A registered platform that lets people give, manage and withdraw consent. |
| Data Protection Board of India | The regulator that inquires into breaches and imposes penalties. |

Full definitions are in [key DPDPA definitions](/docs/dpdpa-key-definitions).

## What organisations must do

In short, a Data Fiduciary must:

1. Have a lawful basis: the person's **consent**, or one of the listed **legitimate uses** ([consent and legitimate uses](/docs/dpdpa-consent-and-legitimate-uses)).
2. Give a clear **notice** when asking for consent ([notice requirements](/docs/dpdpa-notice-requirements)).
3. Keep data accurate, protect it with **reasonable security safeguards**, and erase it when it's no longer needed ([obligations of Data Fiduciaries](/docs/dpdpa-data-fiduciary-obligations)).
4. Report **personal data breaches** to the Board and to each affected person ([breach response](/docs/dpdpa-personal-data-breach)).
5. Honour people's **rights**: access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal and nomination ([rights and duties](/docs/dpdpa-data-principal-rights)).
6. Get **verifiable parental consent** before processing children's data ([children's data](/docs/dpdpa-childrens-data)).

## Penalties

The Schedule to the Act sets penalties of up to **₹250 crore** per instance for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, and up to ₹200 crore for failing to report a breach or meet the duties on children's data. See [penalties and the Data Protection Board](/docs/dpdpa-penalties).
