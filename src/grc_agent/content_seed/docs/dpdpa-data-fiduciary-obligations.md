**Section 8** sets out the general Data Fiduciary obligations that apply to every organisation deciding how personal data is used. They apply on top of the rules on [consent](/docs/dpdpa-consent-and-legitimate-uses) and [notice](/docs/dpdpa-notice-requirements).

## You stay responsible (Section 8(1))

A Data Fiduciary is responsible for complying with the Act for any processing done by it **or on its behalf by a Data Processor**, whatever its contract says, and even if the Data Principal fails to perform their own duties.

## Processors need a valid contract (Section 8(2))

You may engage a Data Processor only **under a valid contract**. List every vendor that touches personal data, then put a data processing agreement in place with each.

## Accuracy (Section 8(3))

If personal data is used to make a decision that affects the person, or is disclosed to another Data Fiduciary, you must ensure it's **complete, accurate and consistent**.

## Technical and organisational measures (Section 8(4))

Implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to make sure the Act and Rules are followed. In practice: policies, training, assigned owners and records.

## Reasonable security safeguards (Section 8(5))

Protect personal data in your possession or control, including data your processors handle, with **reasonable security safeguards** to prevent a personal data breach. Failing to do so carries the highest penalty in the Act: up to **₹250 crore**. The Rules list minimum measures, including:

- encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens
- access control
- logging and monitoring of access, with logs kept for detection and investigation
- backups and continuity measures
- matching obligations in contracts with Data Processors

## Report breaches (Section 8(6))

If a personal data breach happens, inform the **Data Protection Board** and **each affected Data Principal**. See [personal data breach response](/docs/dpdpa-personal-data-breach).

## Erase data when it's no longer needed (Section 8(7) and 8(8))

Unless the law requires you to keep it, erase personal data, and make your processors erase it:

- when the Data Principal **withdraws consent**, or
- as soon as it's reasonable to assume the **purpose is no longer served**

Under Section 8(8), the purpose is treated as no longer served if the person doesn't approach you or exercise their rights for a prescribed period. The Rules set that period for certain large classes of platform.

## Publish a contact (Section 8(9))

Publish the business contact information of a **Data Protection Officer**, if you have one, or of a person who can answer Data Principals' questions about processing.

## Grievance redressal (Section 8(10))

Set up an effective mechanism to redress Data Principals' grievances. See [rights and duties of Data Principals](/docs/dpdpa-data-principal-rights).
