The DPDPA definitions are in **Section 2** of the Act. These are the ones that come up in almost every engagement.

## People and organisations

**Data Principal** (Section 2(j)): the individual the personal data relates to. For a **child** (anyone under 18), it includes the parent or lawful guardian. For a person with a disability who has a lawful guardian acting for them, it includes that guardian.

**Data Fiduciary** (Section 2(i)): any person who, alone or with others, decides the purpose and means of processing personal data. If you decide *why* data is used, you're a Data Fiduciary.

**Data Processor** (Section 2(k)): any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary. A payroll provider, CRM vendor or cloud host is usually a Data Processor.

**Significant Data Fiduciary** (Section 2(z)): a Data Fiduciary, or class of them, notified by the government under Section 10 because of the volume or sensitivity of data it processes and the risks involved. See [Significant Data Fiduciaries](/docs/dpdpa-significant-data-fiduciary).

**Consent Manager** (Section 2(g)): a person registered with the Data Protection Board who acts as a single point of contact for Data Principals to give, manage, review and withdraw consent through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform.

**Board** (Section 2(c)): the Data Protection Board of India, set up under Section 18.

## Data and processing

**Personal data** (Section 2(t)): any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to that data. There's no separate category of "sensitive" personal data in the Act, though children's data gets extra protection.

**Digital personal data** (Section 2(n)): personal data in digital form. The Act applies to this.

**Processing** (Section 2(x)): a wholly or partly automated operation on digital personal data, including collection, recording, organisation, storage, use, sharing, disclosure, erasure and destruction.

**Personal data breach** (Section 2(u)): any unauthorised processing of personal data, or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to it, that compromises its confidentiality, integrity or availability. A lost laptop, a misdirected email and ransomware can all be breaches.

**Child** (Section 2(f)): an individual who has not completed 18 years of age. See [children's data](/docs/dpdpa-childrens-data).

**Consent**: not defined in Section 2, but Section 6 sets out what valid consent is. See [consent and legitimate uses](/docs/dpdpa-consent-and-legitimate-uses).

> Clause letters in Section 2 are given for convenience. Check them against the gazetted text before citing them in a client document.
