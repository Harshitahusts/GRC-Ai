DPDPA compliance for small businesses is no longer optional. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to **every** organisation that handles digital personal data, whatever its size, and most of its obligations take full effect on **13 May 2027**. There's no turnover threshold and no blanket exemption for SMEs.

The good news: for most small and mid-sized businesses, getting compliant is a matter of organisation, not expensive technology. This guide explains what applies to you and gives you a plan.

## Does the DPDPA apply to my business?

Almost certainly, yes. If you collect names, phone numbers, email addresses, payment details or ID numbers from customers or employees, and you store them digitally (a CRM, a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp Business account, a cloud drive), you're a **Data Fiduciary** under the Act. See the [DPDPA overview](/docs/dpdpa-overview) for who's covered and the [key definitions](/docs/dpdpa-key-definitions).

The government can exempt some start-ups from certain obligations, but that needs a specific notification. Don't assume it covers you.

## What's at stake

The Data Protection Board can impose penalties of up to **₹250 crore** for failing to protect personal data, and up to ₹200 crore for failing to report a breach. Those are maximums, and the Board must weigh proportionality, but a single incident can still be fatal for a small business. See [DPDPA penalties](/docs/dpdpa-penalties).

The more immediate pressure often comes from **larger customers**. Enterprises that must comply themselves are already asking their suppliers for data processing agreements and security answers. Being ready early wins deals.

## The 8 steps to DPDPA compliance

### 1. Map your data

Write down what personal data you collect, why, where it's stored, and which tools and vendors touch it. This one document drives everything else. Include the tools people forget: email marketing, accounting software, HR and payroll, and messaging apps.

### 2. Pick a lawful basis for each purpose

For each purpose, you need either the person's **consent** or one of the narrow **legitimate uses** in Section 7, such as employment purposes or legal obligations. Marketing almost always needs consent. See [consent and legitimate uses](/docs/dpdpa-consent-and-legitimate-uses).

### 3. Fix your notices and consent

Show a clear [notice](/docs/dpdpa-notice-requirements) wherever you collect data, listing what you collect and why. Replace pre-ticked boxes with an unticked, per-purpose opt-in, and make withdrawing consent as easy as giving it.

### 4. Put basic security in place

"Reasonable security safeguards" for a small business usually means: access limited to the people who need it, multi-factor authentication, encryption on laptops and cloud storage, logs you can review, and tested backups. See [Data Fiduciary obligations](/docs/dpdpa-data-fiduciary-obligations).

### 5. Write a breach playbook

Decide now who does what if data leaks. You must tell the Data Protection Board and every affected person, and send the Board a detailed report within 72 hours. A two-page [breach playbook](/docs/dpdpa-personal-data-breach) with templates saves you when it matters.

### 6. Sign contracts with your vendors

Every vendor that handles personal data for you needs a valid contract covering how they protect it and when they delete it. Check where they store data, too: see [cross-border data transfer](/docs/dpdpa-cross-border-data-transfer).

### 7. Set retention periods and delete old data

Decide how long you keep each type of data, and delete it when the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless a law requires you to keep it. Old data you don't need is pure risk.

### 8. Handle rights requests and complaints

Publish a contact for data questions, and a simple way for people to ask for their data, correct it or delete it, and to complain. Log each request. See [Data Principal rights](/docs/dpdpa-data-principal-rights).

## If you serve children

If any of your customers could be under 18, as in education, gaming or consumer apps, you need **verifiable parental consent** and must not track children or target ads at them. This carries a penalty of up to ₹200 crore. See [children's data](/docs/dpdpa-childrens-data).

## How long does it take?

For a typical small business with a handful of systems, the steps above take a few weeks of focused work spread over two or three months. Start with the data map: it will tell you where your real gaps are.
